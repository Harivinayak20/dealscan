## Description (gallery / product description, 2-3 sentences)

Dealscan is a free tool for used-car buyers. Paste any listing and get a fair-price verdict, plus a plain-English breakdown of the dealer fees you are being charged. No signup, no account, nothing to install.

Extras: a dealer fee glossary, 28 model comparisons, "years to avoid" pages for common models, an embeddable price-checker widget, and a new 50-state data study on dealer doc fees.

## First comment (maker story, honest tone)

Post immediately at launch from the maker account.

---
Hi Product Hunt,

I built Dealscan because used-car shopping is full of numbers that are hard to sanity-check. You find a listing, and you have no fast way to know if the price is fair or if the dealer fees stacked on top are normal or padded.

So Dealscan does two simple things: you paste a listing and it gives you a fair-price verdict, and it breaks down the dealer fees in plain English so you can tell which ones are standard and which are markup. It is free, there is no signup, and there is nothing to install.

While building the fee side I kept hitting a wall: nobody had a clear picture of how much dealer doc fees actually vary. So I pulled the numbers for all 50 states. Doc fees range about 11x, from around $85 in California to as high as $999 in Florida, and only 16 states cap them. Every one of the ten highest-fee states has no cap. That study is live at /research/dealer-fees-by-state.

I would love feedback on the fair-price verdict accuracy and on the fee breakdown - if you run a real listing through it and something looks off, tell me. That is the part I most want to get right.

Thanks for taking a look.
---

## Hunter outreach shortlist

The hunter model is NOT dead in 2026, but it has compressed. Product Hunt has stated there is no algorithmic preference for third-party hunters vs self-submission, and roughly 44% of products are now self-hunted, including a majority of #1 Product of the Day winners historically. A misaligned hunter is worth zero or negative; a good one in your niche is worth maybe 50-150 incremental upvotes on launch day.

Recommendation for Dealscan: self-hunt. It is a free consumer tool with a clear demo and a data-study hook that carries itself. There is no clear "big consumer-tool hunter" whose endorsement would meaningfully move a free car tool, and chasing one risks a bad-fit match. Self-hunting is standard and carries no penalty.

If the owner still wants a hunter: don't hire from paid "hunter marketplace" lists (low-quality, can look like vote-buying). Instead, before launch week, browse Product Hunt's own active-hunters discussion threads and its live leaderboards, find someone who has personally hunted consumer / shopping / auto tools in the last 60 days, and send a genuine 2-minute-yes note with the assets above. Verify recency yourself - do not rely on any static "top hunters" listicle, most are stale. If no active, on-topic, recently-visible hunter turns up, skip it and self-hunt.
